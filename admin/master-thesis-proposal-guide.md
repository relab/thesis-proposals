# What Makes a Good Master's Thesis Project Description?

*A master's thesis is a scientific work.*

Scientific means that it should ask and answer a research question.
The short time you have for writing a thesis typically will not allow you to invent new things or solve entirely new problems.
A typical research question is of the form:

*How much does methodology/technology X improve metric M when applied to problem Y?*

In your project description you should make clear that your research question is at the frontier of science, how you want to evaluate it, and why it is a plausible approach. 
Finally, you should plan a flexible scope in case things turn out harder or easier than expected.
Use the [project description template](master-thesis-description-template.md) as a starting point.

## Frontier of Science

Being at the **frontier of science** means that the thesis should be related to the scientific state of the art.
One way to show this is to apply and evaluate a method or technology that is a recent development.
Ideally, you should cite a recent research paper, typically published within the last five years, that proposes the method you want to apply.

## Evaluation

**Evaluation** should quantify, or otherwise substantiate with evidence, the answer to your research question.
Note that to know whether an evaluation result is good or bad often requires a baseline, which you may have to implement as well.
For example, to evaluate the research question *How much does explicit memory management reduce the peak memory footprint of a replicated state machine?*
you may have to measure and compare the memory footprint of one system implemented with explicit memory management with one without it (e.g., garbage collected).
In your project description, you need to explain how you can evaluate your research question and what the baseline should be.

## Plausibility

Finally, your project description should explain why it is **plausible** that your method may lead to a satisfactory solution for your problem.
A thesis may not always produce a positive result, and the evaluation should be performed with an open mind.
Still, to justify investing in the evaluation, a positive result should be plausible.

## Flexible scope

It is often difficult to grasp how feasible a master's thesis is.
You should therefore plan ahead, what is the minimal necessary work needed to answer the research question? 
You should also consider, what additional features could be relevant if time allows, and what simpler alternative exist, if you fail to complete the necessary work?

## Checklist

Before submitting your project description, check that it answers:

- [ ] **Research question:** Does it name the metric you will measure?
- [ ] **Frontier of science:** Which recent work does the method or technology come from, and is it cited?
- [ ] **Evaluation:** How will you measure the metric, and how will you obtain the baseline?
- [ ] **Plausibility:** Why is a positive result plausible?
- [ ] **Scope:** What can you realistically complete, and what is optional?
